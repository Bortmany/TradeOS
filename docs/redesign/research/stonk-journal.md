# Competitor Intelligence: Stonk Journal (stonkjournal.com)

Research date: 2026-07-12. Method: public web only (marketing site, third-party review/listing sites, feedback board, search-engine snippets). Direct `WebFetch` of stonkjournal.com and most third-party review pages (Trustpilot, GetApp, Capterra, TradingToolsHub, the Sleekplan feedback board, YouTube) returned HTTP 403 (bot/Cloudflare-style protection) on every attempt — findings below are reconstructed from search-engine result snippets that quote or paraphrase those pages, not from directly rendered pages. Confidence is noted per section; treat exact wording as "as reported by search snippets," not verbatim-verified quotes, except where a source is explicitly a search-result quote.

## 1. Positioning & pricing

- Core positioning, per the homepage title tag and repeated snippet copy: **"StonkJournal — #1 Free Trading Journal."** A recurring homepage tagline surfaced in search snippets: **"TRADING JOURNAL FOR THE PEOPLE — NO ADS — NO DATA SOLD — NOT FINANCIAL ADVICE"**, paired with a stat bar: **"EST. 2021 — 10.8M TRADES LOGGED — NO HOT TAKES — JUST RECEIPTS."** ([stonkjournal.com](https://stonkjournal.com/))
- **Two-tier model observed (current/V2 product):**
  - **Free plan** — no credit card, no trial needed, unlimited trades. Reported to include "every core feature": unlimited trades, all market types (stocks, options, futures, forex), stats, P&L calendar, risk rules with a weighted compliance score, multi-account support, trade setups, and share links. ([GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/finance-accounting-software/a/stonk-journal/), [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10005879/Stonk-Journal/))
  - **Pro plan — $10/month** — adds the "AI Coach," CSV import (broker-agnostic, with column mapping), an "all-accounts roll-up," and AI chat/natural-language querying of your own trade data. Marketing framing per a snippet: this costs "3-4x" less than "a level of behavioral analysis and natural language querying" on competing platforms. ([stonkjournal.com/articles/best-free-trading-journals-2026](https://stonkjournal.com/articles/best-free-trading-journals-2026))
- **Historical/legacy note:** older search results and a Trustpilot snippet describe Stonk Journal as funded purely by **voluntary donations, "no subscriptions, no ads, no data sales,"** and reference a "V1" product for which support has ended, with users pushed to migrate to "V2" (the currently marketed Free/Pro product above). This suggests the free-donation-only model is the product's original (2021-era) identity and the $10/mo Pro tier is a newer monetization layer bolted onto a rebuilt V2. **Could not verify** the exact date of the V1→V2 transition or whether donations are still solicited alongside Pro. ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/stonkjournal.com))
- **Target audience:** messaging skews toward retail/beginner and cost-conscious active traders — "trading journal for the people," free-forever framing, explicit contrast against paid incumbents (see the "TraderSync alternative" positioning page, title only verifiable: [stonkjournal.com/an-elegant-and-free-tradersync-alternative](https://stonkjournal.com/an-elegant-and-free-tradersync-alternative/)). Asset-class coverage (stocks, options, futures, forex, with proper contract multipliers) and multi-account support (paper, live, futures, prop-firm) suggest they also court more serious/funded traders, not purely beginners — this is broader than the "simple beginner journal" reputation TradeOS's brief assumes. **Coverage is thin** on any explicit "who this is for" statement; inferred from feature/marketing mix, not a direct quote.

## 5. In-app UI patterns worth noting

- **Nav model:** described as "dashboard-first, trade log front and center" — suggesting a persistent dashboard/trade-log-centric structure rather than a deeply nested multi-page nav. **Could not verify** actual sidebar/tab structure.
- **Dashboard composition:** stats (win rate, profit factor, avg win/loss, hold time) + equity curve (sliceable by symbol, day-of-week, drawdown) + a P&L calendar/heatmap for month-at-a-glance review. This combination (KPI row + equity curve + calendar heatmap) matches a fairly standard trading-journal dashboard pattern also used by competitors like TradeZella/TradesViz.
- **P&L calendar/heatmap:** explicitly called out as a distinguishing feature in marketing copy ("a P&L heatmap tells you when you trade well — and when you should go for a walk instead") — framed with personality/voice, not just a data widget. Worth noting as a copy pattern (injecting humor/self-awareness into a data view).
- **Rules/compliance UI:** rule violations are flagged "as you build the trade" (i.e., real-time, at trade-entry time, not just in post-hoc analytics) and trades get a **weighted compliance score**. This real-time-at-entry flagging is a meaningfully different interaction model than a purely retrospective "grade" — worth comparing directly against TradeOS's own entry flow.
- **Color semantics / density / chart styles:** **could not verify** — no accessible screenshot or video frame description was obtainable via search snippets alone.
- **Multi-account switching:** supports paper, live, futures, and prop-firm accounts "kept separate so they don't bleed into each other" — implies an account-switcher UI pattern, common in this category.

## 7. Verdict

**Three things TradeOS should replicate:**
1. **A rules/compliance engine that flags violations in real time at trade-entry, not just after the fact, feeding a weighted per-trade score** — Stonk Journal already ships this on its *free* tier ("10 rule types... real-time compliance flags... weighted scoring tells you which trades followed your plan"). TradeOS's discipline score should make sure it matches or beats this on explainability and real-time feedback, since a free competitor already has a credible version of the exact mechanic TradeOS is centered on.
2. **Privacy-first, revocable share links** (opt-in, regenerate-to-kill-old-URL, adjustable anonymity) — cheap to build, high trust value, and directly useful for TradeOS's funded/prop audience who may want to prove discipline to a prop firm or peer without exposing full account details.
3. **Multi-account separation that explicitly names prop-firm accounts as a first-class account type** (alongside paper/live/futures) — validates that TradeOS's prop/funded-trader focus is a real, demanded segmentation, and the "kept separate so they don't bleed into each other" framing is worth matching or improving on (e.g., per-account rule sets, not just per-account data isolation).

**Three things to avoid:**
1. **Free-but-manual-entry-first friction** — Stonk Journal's most consistent complaint is "no import, everything is one-by-one," and even its own Pro CSV import is a manual, non-real-time workaround (no live broker sync yet, confirmed still on their own public roadmap board). TradeOS should not let import/sync quality lag behind its scoring sophistication — a discipline score is only as good as the data feeding it.
2. **Weak documentation for a feature-dense product** — reviewers explicitly cite a steep learning curve and no adequate help files despite liking the product otherwise. TradeOS's "dense, terminal-style" UI is even more exposed to this risk than Stonk Journal's, so onboarding/help content needs to be treated as a first-class deliverable, not an afterthought.
3. **Data-integrity bugs in the core ledger** (the reported "trades disappear and reappear" glitch) — for a discipline/scoring product especially, any doubt about whether the underlying trade log is accurate destroys the credibility of the score built on top of it. Trust in the ledger must be treated as sacred, more so than for a plain journal.

**One gap TradeOS could own:**
Stonk Journal's rules engine is a real, working competitor to TradeOS's discipline score — but everything found in research describes it as a **generic rule library** (max risk per trade, max position size, max daily loss) rather than a rulebook the trader defines in their own words and that gets tied to *funded-account/prop-firm compliance specifically* (e.g., consistency rules, drawdown-vs-payout math, challenge-phase-aware scoring — the kind of thing dedicated prop tools like TradesViz's prop dashboard or PropTracker specialize in, but that Stonk Journal only weakly touches via account tagging). TradeOS could own **"the discipline score that understands your prop firm's actual rules"** — a scoring system that ingests a specific prop firm's real rulebook (consistency rule, max drawdown, daily loss limit, payout eligibility windows) rather than a generic risk-rule library, positioning the discipline score as pass/fail-relevant to real funded evaluations, not just a generic behavioral nudge. This is a gap Stonk Journal's generic, free-tier rule engine does not appear to fill.
