## 2. New entrants — full pass

### Journali (journali.io)

Confirmed live, positioned explicitly for "futures, options & prop firms." Runs **pre-trade rule checks** ("TradeCheck" gives a setup a red/green light before entry), **mid-session tilt detection** (flags a revenge trade as it happens), and **prop-rule breach warnings that fire before the breach**, against 10+ pre-configured firm rulebooks (Apex, Topstep, FTMO, MyFundedFutures, FundedNext, Tradeify, and others). Multi-account cross-firm dashboard with per-account analytics and AI insights. Pro tier ($20/mo) adds unlimited accounts, full rule tracking, and "Goals & Rules with consistency scoring." [Certain] this product exists and targets the same prop-firm-rules-enforcement niche as TradeOS. [Guessing — could not verify from snippets] whether "consistency scoring" is a unified 0–100 explainable score with a visible per-rule arithmetic breakdown, or a looser compliance indicator; the marketing language stops short of TradeOS's specific claim (deterministic, auditable, per-trade grade against *your own* rulebook).

**This is the more direct positioning threat of the two new entrants**, because it's the first competitor found (beyond Tradingtick/Temper, covered in the July report) that does real-time, *pre-trade* rule enforcement — closer to TradeOS's "before it costs a payout" tagline than TradeOS's own architecture currently is (see Gap #1 below).

### Tradoshi (tradoshi.com)

Confirmed live; an Android app release on August 27, 2026 generated a burst of syndicated coverage. Free plan (no card, no time limit) includes 1 auto-synced broker account and — unlike TradeOS's free Starter — **the discipline score itself, the "Oshi Score," free**. The Oshi Score is a 0–100 figure computed across six axes: win rate, profit factor, average-win-vs-average-loss, recovery factor, drawdown control, and consistency. Essential ($19.99/mo) and Pro ($39.99/mo, multi-prop-firm + challenge tracking) tiers add AI coaching and more broker accounts.

**Important distinction for positioning:** based on the available description, the Oshi Score reads as a **results-based aggregate** (win rate, profit factor, drawdown, etc. — the same shape as Tradezella's Zella Score) rather than a **per-trade audit against the trader's own written rulebook**. That is the specific mechanism TradeOS's discipline score uses and the July report identified as unclaimed. [Likely, based on the described six axes] Tradoshi's score does not do rule-by-rule pass/fail grading the way TradeOS's rule engine does — but this could not be fully verified without product access, and Tradoshi also ships an AI coach, which cuts against TradeOS's "no AI, fully deterministic" contrast.

## 3. User sentiment (Trustpilot/review-aggregator signals — Reddit unreachable)

Reddit `site:reddit.com` searches for Tradezella, TraderSync, and Edgewonk complaints returned no usable Reddit content across five distinct query attempts. Sentiment below is from Trustpilot rating aggregates and third-party review summaries instead.

- **Tradezella** — 4.8/5 on Trustpilot, 988 reviews, 91% five-star (as of July 31, 2026). 93% of positive reviews praise support specifically. But **37% of negative reviews cite bugs/technical issues** — recurring pattern: broker sync failures, trades not appearing, API token expiry (Schwab and Interactive Brokers named specifically), data inconsistencies. Paraphrase: "support is excellent at responding — but the product itself has reliability issues support alone can't fix." Reaffirms the July report's #1 complaint theme. [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tradezella.com)
- **TraderSync** — Trustpilot rating reported inconsistently across sources as 4.4–4.7/5 from ~312 reviews, ~90% five-star (worth a manual spot-check before quoting externally). Recurring negative pattern: "broker synchronization often gets stuck and keeps loading," slow servers, trades stuck showing as open requiring a manual counter-trade to close, and one paraphrased complaint that it's "a very expensive journal but with consistently wrong data" with slow support replies. Positive pattern: replay mode and edge-detection praised. [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/tradersync.com)
- **Edgewonk** — 4.7 TrustScore but from only ~40 reviews (small sample, flag accordingly), 97% five-star. Analytics depth is the #1 praised theme (65%). Complaint pattern (from a dedicated "complaints" article): manual CSV import friction for non-MetaTrader brokers, no native mobile app, steep learning curve, and resentment over the move from a one-time license to an annual subscription. [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/edgewonk.com), [Modest Money complaints roundup](https://www.modestmoney.com/edgewonk-complaints-and-negative-ratings/)

**Repeated pattern across all three legacy leaders:** broker-sync reliability is still the dominant technical complaint category-wide, unchanged from July. TradeOS's read-only, AES-256-encrypted, adapter-based connectors plus honest sync-status UI remains correctly positioned — reaffirmed, not walked back, by this refresh.

## 5. Two strategic assessments

### (a) Does TradeOS's $29/$79 under- or over-cut Tradezella's current ladder?

**Mixed, and shifted since July.** Tradezella is now three tiers ($35/$59/$99), and — critically — **Prop Firm Sync moved from a premium-gated feature to something bundled into every paid tier, including the $35 entry tier.**

- TradeOS Pro ($29, no prop tracker) undercuts Tradezella Essential ($35, includes prop sync) by ~17% — but Essential now includes something Pro doesn't.
- TradeOS Elite ($79, adds prop tracking) sits between Tradezella Pro ($59) and Ultra ($99) — but a Tradezella customer who only wants prop guardrails gets them at $35, cheaper than TradeOS Pro, let alone Elite.
- **TradeOS's ladder tracks TraderSync almost dollar-for-dollar** ($29.95/$49.95/$79.95 vs TradeOS's $29/—/$79) more closely than Tradezella.
- The real pressure is from below: **Journali ($20/mo), Tradoshi ($19.99–$39.99/mo), and TradesViz ($19.99–$29.99/mo) — the products most similar in positioning to TradeOS — all undercut Pro by 30–45%.** TradeOS is priced like the legacy leaders but positioned like the new discipline-score entrants, who are priced well below it.

**[Certain]** TradeOS is not the cheap option in its own positioning category anymore, even though it undercuts the two biggest legacy names feature-for-feature at entry tier. Worth a deliberate pricing decision.

### (b) Is "explainable discipline score" still unclaimed territory?

**More contested than in July, but the specific combination TradeOS ships still appears unclaimed — lower confidence than three months ago.**

- **Tradoshi**'s free "Oshi Score" reads as a **results-based aggregate** (win rate, profit factor, drawdown, etc.) — the same shape as Zella Score — not a per-trade, per-rule audit against the trader's *own* rulebook. [Likely, not fully verified]
- **Journali** now ships real-time, pre-trade rule enforcement against actual prop-firm rulebooks plus a "consistency score" — closer to the *enforcement* half of TradeOS's own tagline than TradeOS's current after-the-fact architecture. [Certain] a real, named product now doing part of what TradeOS claims as its edge.
- Combined with July's findings (Tradingtick, Temper, Tradezella's "Rule Adherence Score"), the count of products circling this territory has grown from 2–3 to 4–5 by September.

**[Likely]** The precise combination — deterministic, per-trade, per-rule breakdown against the trader's own custom rulebook, arithmetic shown — is still not confirmed elsewhere. But "genuinely unclaimed territory" is no longer the safest framing; "most complete version of a crowding category" is more defensible, and the pre-trade-enforcement gap is the one piece of TradeOS's own tagline a named competitor (Journali) already does better today.
